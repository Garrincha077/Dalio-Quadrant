# Data priorities after the expanded-period checks

The earlier IEF result (+5.45 pp, two episodes) was a worthwhile hypothesis.
Independent longer ETF histories change training/model choices and do not retain
that result. Better data should improve comparability and the strength of the
test; they are not expected to mechanically raise measured returns.

## Priorities

| Priority | Needed data | Why it matters | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Observed Treasury total-return indices or security-level prices, coupons, accrued interest, maturity, duration and convexity | Replaces the reconstructed long bond history and distinguishes yield carry from duration exposure | Document reinvestment, maturity bucket, index rule changes and availability; match IEF's 7–10Y exposure or label a separate universe |
| 2 | Archived initial yield and portfolio-duration histories | Links an actual entry yield to the risk of the asset being bought | Use dates/release cutoffs, not today's fund factsheet as a historical constant; retain original quoted/clean/dirty price conventions |
| 3 | Original macro publication archives where ALFRED coverage is short | Could extend genuinely contemporaneous macro tests beyond the current May-2011 archive boundary | Verify published CFNAI/PCE values, reference periods and timestamps; no reconstruction from today's revisions masquerading as an original signal |
| 4 | Additional countries/markets with local yields, inflation and currency returns | Adds different cycles for checking a rule outside the US sample | Predeclare markets; distinguish local-currency, USD and hedged results; keep duration/asset definitions comparable |
| 5 | Daily return paths, spreads and realistic execution costs | Tests drawdowns and actual execution around a monthly signal | Exclude unavailable intramonth information; daily observations do not create additional independent macro cycles |

Examples of sources to assess for the first priority include CRSP US Treasury
security data and documented Treasury total-return index families from established
index providers. These are procurement/source-evaluation options, not data already
obtained or verified here. Availability, terms and cost must be established before
use; no paid service has been purchased or accessed by this work.

## Test design alongside the data upgrade

1. Fix a small number of economically interpretable hypotheses before the next
   evaluation, including the role of initial yield and duration. Historical fitting
   is legitimate; the next evaluation must be distinct from the data used to choose
   that hypothesis.
2. Retain base/yield/trend stages on exactly the same eligible dates and assets.
   Evaluate continuous rankings as well as sparse positive/top-quartile entry masks.
   A selected-return average does not establish portfolio outperformance.
3. Check stability by fixed eras and comparable market regimes, then on separately
   declared markets or later prospective periods. Do not pick a favourable era
   after inspecting it and call that an independent result.
4. Report episode concentration, non-overlapping holdings, downside and costs.
   More monthly or daily rows are not more independent economic cycles.

The current extension already improves source coverage, actual trade dates,
per-asset availability and common-calendar checks. It does not supply observed
long Treasury index returns, historical fund-duration records or pre-2011 original
macro vintages. Those limitations remain beside the associated results.
