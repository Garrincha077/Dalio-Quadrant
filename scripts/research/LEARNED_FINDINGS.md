# Learned entry research findings · 26 October 2026

Learning from history is a legitimate research approach. In this limited model family, calibration and continuous responses do not consistently outperform fixed quadrants in later periods. Results depend strongly on the historical information set. No adequately repeated positive result passes the experiment-wide Holm 5% threshold. This does not rule out useful relationships in other model families.

The experiment includes 17 candidates, annual refits, purged earlier validation, three horizons, fixed-quadrant and continuous-model comparisons, and two distinct model-selection objectives. The entry-lift selector was added as a disclosed exploratory amendment after the first mean-error results.

185 actual archived CFNAI/PCE information sets were collected successfully, from May 2011 through September 2026. Vintage models train on macro reconstructions published by their refit date and score each later date from its own snapshot. The fixed two-reference-month lag is retained.

All reported lifts below are cumulative percentage-point differences between selected forward returns and the same asset on all scored dates, after 10 bp per entry/exit side. They are not portfolio CAGR, alpha, or guaranteed prospective returns.

Gold is a clear cautionary example: the learned procedure looks much stronger with revised history than with the historical information sets on the same evaluation dates. The comparison includes different prior validation availability, so the entire difference cannot be attributed solely to one data revision.

For SPY at 12M, the vintage entry-lift selector is modestly above fixed quadrants, but its broad uncertainty does not establish an advantage. Other assets and horizons fail to show a consistent gain. Choosing the best family after viewing these outer results would create another research-selection layer.

## What this research changes

- It demonstrates a complete way to learn from history while respecting the information and label boundaries at each decision. Economic zero/2% anchors remain interpretable reference points; investment-response boundaries are allowed to learn separately.
- The main obstacle is not that historical calibration is inherently invalid. These particular models and candidate-selection procedures have not shown stable, well-supported entry improvement across periods, information sets and assets.
- Accurate average-return forecasts and useful entry rankings are different objectives. Both selectors remain in the record; neither is quietly replaced by the best-looking outer-window family.
- The response map makes the learned relationship inspectable, including flat/unconditional choices, clipping and changing selected boundaries. It is a fitted frontier, not a live recommendation.
- A further, separately declared hypothesis could add starting yields/real rates and asset-market context. That is not tested or implied to work by this experiment.
