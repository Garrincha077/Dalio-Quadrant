Distributions, uncertainty & correlations

Monthly holding windows overlap, especially at 36 months. Intervals use 2,500 paired circular block resamples with blocks of max(12, horizon) months. They describe uncertainty in the difference from the unconditional cohort mean, not the range of the next investment return. Intervals and ranks are exploratory, without multiple-comparison correction. Few economic episodes, revisions and changing monetary regimes limit inference. This study does not retune the quadrant axes or establish a forecasting edge.

Coverage, sources & calculation

Return = adjusted close at entry + horizon / adjusted close at entry − 1. Entry is the reference month end, or two months later. Every intervening monthly price must exist. No costs, tax, financing or leverage. Adjusted prices are a provider total-return proxy. Static axes: CFNAI-MA3 and PCE. Prices: Alpha Vantage monthly adjusted. Constant 2% is a retrospective anchor; the Fed's explicit objective dates from 2012. This snapshot is separate from the frozen momentum investment experiment.

State method, anchors & sources

Static means indicator levels, rather than the three-month changes used by Momentum. The activity axis uses the Chicago Fed's published three-month average of its 85-indicator national activity index. Zero represents growth at its historical trend, not an output gap, zero GDP growth or a recession boundary. Its normalisation can be revised by the publisher.

The inflation axis is 100 × (PCEPI / PCEPI twelve months earlier − 1), minus 2 percentage points. The Fed's 2% longer-run objective refers to headline PCE. Core PCE appears as supporting evidence, with its own label and date. The annual comparison requires the exact matching month; missing bases stay unavailable.

Six monthly activity cross-checks follow categories used by the NBER: industrial production, payroll and household employment, real income excluding transfers, real consumption, and real manufacturing and trade sales. Breadth counts positive annual growth among fresh, completed observations, requires at least four of six, and excludes readings more than two months behind the freshest usable month. Zero is unchanged; missing or stale readings are excluded, with coverage shown. This is our descriptive count, not the NBER's recession-dating method.

These series overlap with the CFNAI and with each other. They explain the economy's composition without being added to the activity axis or treated as independent confirmations. GDP is a quarterly cross-check. Core CPI, shelter, wages and the Dallas Fed's published 12-month trimmed-mean PCE provide inflation detail. Trimmed-mean PCE is already an annual rate; it is not transformed a second time. CPI and wage growth do not share the Fed's headline-PCE target.

The state trail has 12 numbered positions. Monthly, quarterly and annual sampling select reference months; quarterly/annual past buckets require their final month, while the selected ending month may be within an unfinished quarter/year. Missing pairs stay as unconnected gaps. The latest trail ends at the latest indicator reference month, rather than the browser's calendar month. Historical replay changes the chart; the left-hand cards continue to show the latest released economy.

Standard and Close viewports use fixed native-unit scales. Full history expands symmetrically around activity zero and the 2% PCE anchor; selecting a viewport never changes quadrant membership. Diamonds identify out-of-range points, and connecting lines are clipped at the viewport rather than joining displaced diamonds. The direction arrow describes the last adjacent sampled move; overlapping points remain accessible through Inspect position.

Activity context excludes the current reference month and requires at least 24 prior monthly values and 95% reference-window coverage. Robust dispersion = 1.4826 × median absolute deviation from the historical median. Distance from trend = activity / robust dispersion; it is not a mean-centred z-score. Strength = 50 + (50/3) × distance, capped at 0–100. A score of 50 means exactly activity zero; the endpoints mean at least three robust dispersion units from zero, not observed historical minima/maxima or a probability. No mean or percentile recentres the quadrant axes.

Both axes need a completed monthly period and must be no more than 90 days beyond period end, and must describe the same reference month. Buffers of ±0.10 activity-index units and ±0.20 inflation percentage points are descriptive heuristics. They are not fitted to asset returns. GDPNow requires a separately verified update within 21 days and a target quarter no more than 45 days past quarter end; its units are quarter-on-quarter annualised, not GDP YoY.

How the composite works

Each pillar has 25% weight. Inputs are equally weighted within their pillar. Growth weakness uses GDP, production, orders, payrolls and temporary employment with inverted signs. Inflation pressure uses core PCE, CPI, wages and shelter. Financial tightness uses NFCI and bank lending standards with positive signs, and lending growth and net liquidity growth with inverted signs. Debt burden uses household debt service, federal debt/GDP and federal interest/receipts. Fed funds stays in the detailed context because a nominal policy rate alone does not establish restrictiveness.

For the selected reference and estimator, orient each level z-score toward pressure, cap it at ±3, average available inputs within each pillar, then average the four pillars. Score = 50 + (50 / 3) × that average. Capping limits individual outliers here; the detailed heatmap retains uncapped z-scores. Robust mode uses median and scaled MAD, retaining the original observations.

Only fresh inputs with valid reference statistics enter. At least two-thirds of each pillar and 75% of the original, equally weighted pillar coverage are required. Missing inputs receive no neutral value; available inputs share the pillar's fixed weight. All four pillars are required. The same rules independently apply to momentum.

Momentum uses standardized changes over 3 months or 13 weeks; the fiscal ratio uses one year. Building/easing uses ±0.15 z thresholds. The headline score uses levels. Momentum is not a change in the headline score. Below 40 = below usual pressure, 40–60 = near usual, 60–75 = elevated, and 75+ = high. These weights, caps and labels are transparent heuristics, with no forecast calibration. Pillars can offset one another; their spread and inputs remain visible.

All inputs use the latest revised FRED history and their own observation dates. Changing the reference or estimator changes the comparison. This is an independent application model. General methodological reference: OECD/JRC handbook on composite indicators.

Principles before predictions

This independent application is inspired by public All Weather principles. Its formulas are our transparent heuristics, not Ray Dalio's or Bridgewater's proprietary model.

  1. Understand the machine. Separate growth, inflation, credit and the policy response.
  2. Distinguish levels, momentum and expectations. Our axes measure observed momentum. Without a timestamped consensus source, we do not claim to measure surprises.
  3. Balance risks across environments. Equal capital weights do not imply equal risk. Scenario sensitivities are not allocation recommendations.
  4. Make uncertainty visible. Missing, stale and conflicting evidence must be visible before drawing a conclusion.
  5. Write rules that can be challenged. Every score has a source, comparison period, scale and weight.
Exact calculation and quality rules

Annual growth: 100 × (current level / year-earlier level − 1). Monthly and quarterly series require the matching month one year earlier. Weekly lending uses an observation on or before the date 52 weeks earlier, within eight days. A missing comparison is never replaced with an absolute level.

Momentum: current YoY growth minus its value three months earlier (one quarter for GDP). Individual score: clamp(momentum / scale, −1, +1). Axis score: weighted mean of fresh available scores. At least 75% of configured weight must be available on each axis. If either absolute axis score is below 0.15, the result is labelled transition.

Scales, weights, freshness limits and the transition band are explicit descriptive assumptions. A separate vintage-based test of limited calibration variants and predictive models appears in Backtest & prediction. It does not establish a reliable edge or change this chart's scales. Correlated inflation and labour series can still overlap. Manufacturing orders are nominal dollar orders, so inflation can lift that growth input. It is not an independent real-output measure.

Credit conditions, policy rates, net liquidity and stress lights are shown separately. ISM and Roche manual defaults were removed; this version uses only the documented automatic sources.

Reference dates are observation periods, not release dates. Fetch time is recorded separately. Monthly and quarterly freshness ages start at period end; weekly and daily ages start at the observation date. These limits are heuristics, not a release-calendar engine. Historical vintage data and realistic trade timing are required before any backtest.

Scenario sensitivities and important distinctions

Growth accelerating / inflation slowing: equities may benefit. Growth accelerating / inflation accelerating: real assets may benefit. Growth slowing / inflation accelerating: inflation protection may help, depending on real yields. Growth slowing / inflation slowing: nominal duration may help, depending on rates and pricing. None of these relationships is guaranteed.

The arrows indicate growth and inflation momentum, not their absolute levels. Falling momentum does not establish negative inflation or a recession. Earlier labels such as “Overheating” overstated what this calculation could establish and were replaced. A TIPS yield is not a TIPS total return; a Treasury yield change is not a bond return. WTI is a spot price, not a commodity portfolio return. No risk-parity weights are estimated without appropriate return and covariance data.

Long-run statistics and debt-cycle context

Up to 25 years of history is requested for macro and debt indicators. Each current level is compared with 5, 10 and 20 preceding complete calendar years, excluding the current observation. Monthly windows require 60/120/240 observations; quarterly windows 20/40/80; annual fiscal windows 5/10/20. The series must span the entire window and contain at least 95% of expected observations. Short histories or larger gaps are unavailable; smaller gaps retain explicit actual/expected counts.

Standard z = (value − mean) / sample standard deviation (n−1). Robust z = (value − median) / (1.4826 × median absolute deviation). A zero dispersion gives no z-score. Percentiles use the empirical midrank among preceding observations. Pandemic extremes remain in the data; the robust estimator offers an additional comparison.

Levels are annual growth rates for activity and inflation, and published levels or ratios for financial conditions, policy and debt. Momentum is the change over three months (13 weeks for weekly source series; one year for annual fiscal ratios). Weekly statistics are sampled once per completed month and are explicitly dated separately from the latest weekly dashboard value. Annual fiscal series have small samples. Structural changes can shift the reference distribution.

The statistical layer does not replace or retune the quadrant's heuristic weights. Credit context uses TDSP, DRTSCILM, GFDEGDQ188S and 100 × FYOINT / FYFR for matching fiscal years. Debt ratios are not default probabilities. Historical revisions can change z-scores even when the latest observation is unchanged.
